# Yemeni (Ṣanʿāni) curriculum research brief — Pre-A1 → B1

Prepared for Hikaya content authors. Research only; nothing in the repo was edited.
Target variety: **Ṣanʿāni** (urban Sanaa highlands) as the default, with systematic
notes for **Taʿizzi–ʿAdeni** and shorter ones for Ḥaḍrami and Tihāmi.

Transliteration used below: `g` = ق, `j` = ج, `θ` = ث, `ð` = ذ, `ḍ` = ض, `ṭ` = ط,
`ẓ` = ظ, `ṣ` = ص, `ḥ` = ح, `ʕ` = ع, `ʔ` = ء, `š` = ش, `x` = خ, `ġ` = غ, long vowels
`ā ī ū`. Where a form is quoted from Qafisheh, his romanisation is normalised to
this scheme (his OCR renders ð as `é`, š as `f/s`, ʕ as `9/Q`, θ as `6/8`, ġ as `G`).

Every Arabic form in this brief is dialectal. The only MSA-shaped tokens that
appear are هذا/هذه (tolerated for Yemeni in `msaLeakDetector.ts`) and, where
explicitly labelled, classical survivals that Sanaa genuinely keeps (أين، ما،
اللذي) — these are flagged so authors can decide whether to *teach* them or
merely *recognise* them.

## 2. Variety to teach and why

**Teach Ṣanʿāni.** Reasons, in order of weight:

1. **It is what the documentation describes.** Every full-length pedagogical or descriptive work on Yemeni Arabic — Qafisheh's *Yemeni Arabic I–II* and *Reference Grammar* (all "based on the dialect of Sanʿa"), Watson's *Syntax* and *Ṣbaḥtū!*, the Alshargi/Habash Sanaani corpus — is Ṣanʿāni. Authors can check forms against S1/S2 line by line. Nothing comparable exists for Taʿizz; Aden has Feghali 1991 only.
2. **It is the prestige urban variety of the capital and the register of most Yemeni YouTube comedy** the app has already seeded (Mustafa Al-Mawmari, Ahmed Hajar, HMADA are Sanaa creators; see migration `20260823090500`). Wikipedia puts Sanʿani at ~13 M speakers and Taʿizzi-ʿAdeni at ~12 M (S6, S7) — a near tie, so the media/documentation argument decides it.
3. **It is conservative in a way that helps learners**: interdentals kept, hamza kept, classical `mā`/`ʔayn`/`sāra` still alive (S6, S1). A learner who later meets MSA or Gulf Arabic loses nothing.
4. The repo already commits to it: `dialectSubvarieties.ts` lists `sanaani` first with the hint "ق as [g], ج kept as [dʒ]; the bare-perfect and the ـش negation are the giveaways", and `YEMENI_IDENTITY` names صنعاء القديمة.

**Caveat authors must internalise:** the Lisan corpus is Twitter, pan-Yemeni and unlabelled for sub-dialect (docs/lisan-yemeni-corpus.md), so its counts reflect what *written, national* Yemeni looks like — heavy on Taʿizz/Aden and on Egyptian/Gulf media forms (فين 428, ده 1021, ليه 310, كده 130). Where Lisan and Qafisheh disagree (e.g. "now": Lisan الحين 418 > ذلحين 58 > ذحين 9; Qafisheh ðalḥīn ≈58 tokens, الحين 0), **Qafisheh wins for what to teach as Ṣanʿāni; Lisan tells you what to accept as Yemeni.**

**Stress and rhythm.** Ṣanʿāni has strong, clearly placed stress and reduces unstressed short vowels to [ə] (S6). Qafisheh's perfect paradigm marks the stress explicitly: `dáras / dárasu / dárasat` (stem stressed), `darást / darástu / darásti / darásna` (suffix syllable stressed), `darasáyn / darastáyn` (final). Geminates are phonemic and drilled (كلّنا kul-luna, مسّاش massāš). Teach learners to *hold* doubled consonants and to hear the stressed syllable before the segments.

## 4. Grammar inventory, ordered A1 → A2 → B1

Category ids are the six in `grammarTaxonomy.ts`: `verb-conjugation`, `pronouns`,

are Lisan (`docs/yemeni/lexicon-full.json` unless marked *markers*); **Q:** are

Ordering principle (from S1's own unit order and the CEFR A1 goals): equational

### A1 (Stage 1–early Stage 2)

#### 4.1 Subject pronouns and the equational sentence — `pronouns`, `sentence-structure`
Ṣanʿāni set (Q Unit 1–4; Watson):

| | sg | pl |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **أنا** ana | **إحنا** iḥna (L: احنا 771; حنا 29) |
| 2m | **أنت** ant (Sanaa often *ant* with no final vowel; Aden *anta*) | **أنتو** antu (L 361) |
| 2f | **أنتي** anti | **أنتين** antayn (fem. pl. — Sanaa keeps it; Q lists ‘antayn) |
| 3m | **هو** hū/huw | **هم** hum |
| 3f | **هي** hī/hiy | **هن** hin (fem. pl., Q; L هن 30) |

Examples:
- أنا من صنعاء، وأنت من أين؟ — *ana min ṣanʕā, w-ant min ʔayn?* — "I'm from Sanaa, and where are you from?" (Q Unit 1 text: *'ana min al-yaman. wa 'ant min 'ayn?*)
- هي مهندسة وهو طبيب. — *hī muhandiseh w-hū ṭabīb.* — "She's an engineer and he's a doctor."
- إحنا كلنا بخير. — *iḥna kulluna bi-xayr.* — "We're all fine." (Q Unit 3)

Traps: (a) feminine plurals **أنتين / هن** and the verb ending **-ayn** (تشتين، درسن→درسين) are alive in Sanaa but fading in the cities; teach for recognition at A2, never mark a learner wrong for using أنتو/هم. (b) Aden splits **أنا (m.) / أني (f.)** (S3) — recognition note only. (c) Respect plural: Sanaa uses **أنتو / حضرتكم** to elders and strangers (S8) — teach with greetings.

#### 4.2 Possessive/object suffixes; عند / مع / لـ — `possessives`
Suffix set (Q Unit 4, normalised):

| | after consonant | after vowel | on verbs (object) |
|---|---|---|---|
| my | **-ī** بيتي | -ya/-y | **-nī** شافني |
| your m. | **-ak** بيتك | -k | -ak |
| your f. | **-iš** بيتش، كيف حالش، عندش | -š | -iš |
| his | **-a / -uh** بيته (Q: *fuġla* "his work") | -h | -a/-uh |
| her | **-ha / -aha** بيتها | -ha | -ha |
| our | **-na / -ana** بيتنا | -na | -na |
| your pl. | **-kum / -akum** بيتكم | -kum | -kum |
| your f.pl. | **-kin** بيتكن | -kin | -kin |
| their | **-hum / -ahum** بيتهم | -hum | -hum |
| their f. | **-hin** بيتهن | -hin | -hin |

"Have":
- **عند** + suffix (Q Unit 8: *ʕindī, ʕindak, ʕindiš, ʕinda(h), ʕindaha, ʕindana, ʕindakum, ʕindahum*; L عندي 165, عندك 316): عندي موعد — *ʕindī mawʕid* — "I have an appointment"; عندك قهوة؟ — إي، كثير. — *ʕindak gahwa? — ʔī, kaθīr* — "Do you have coffee? — Yes, lots." (Q)
- **مع** for things on one's person / with: معي زلط — *maʕī zalaṭ* — "I've got money on me"; معاك سيارة؟ (Q: *maʕ → maʕā-* before most suffixes: معاه، معاها، معاكم، معانا, but **معي، معش**).
- **لـ** for relations/obligations: لك أخ؟ — *lak ax?* — "Have you got a brother?"; لي عنده مئة ريال — "he owes me 100 riyals" [own knowledge].
- Negation: **ما عنديش / ما عندناش** — Q Unit 59: *ma ʕindakš jahhāl?* "Don't you have children?", *ma ʕindināš gāt* "We don't have qat."

Traps: **-iš** for "your (f.)" is the Sanaa/north form; the detector's normaliser won't confuse it with anything, but TTS may read بيتش oddly — vocalise بَيْتِش. In Aden it is **-ik** (S3 *haqqik*). The Sanaa 3ms suffix is often written ـه but heard as **-a** after a consonant (Q *fuġla* "his work", *ʔisma* "his name").

#### 4.3 Existence فيه / بِه — ما فيش / مابش; كان فيه — `negation`, `sentence-structure`
Two words for "there is": **فيه** *fīh* (pan-Yemeni; Q Unit 24 "fii as a Verb": *fii ʔaḥad?* "Is there anyone?") and the Ṣanʿāni **بِه** *bih* (Q Unit 59: "verb-like expressions … *bih* 'there is; there are'"; Q counts bih 97×, fii 80×).
- فيه شاهي؟ — *fīh šāhī?* — "Is there tea?"
- **ما فيش** *mā fīš* / **مابش** *mābiš* — Q: *maabiš mēʔ* "There is no water", *maabiš ʔasʕār muḥaddada* "There are no fixed prices"; L: مافيش 100, مافي 139 (*markers*), مابش 9, ماش 4.
- Past: **كان فيه / كان به** — Q Unit 30: *kaan fii film* "There was a movie"; negative **ما كانش فيه**: *ma kaanš fii nās* "There were no people."

Trap: **مافيش** is on the *forbidden* list harvested from the approved corpus rules (migration 20260804150000 lists مافيش/ماكانش/ماعنديش as "Egyptian negations" now blocked for Yemeni). The rule was written from Twitter code-switching; but ما فيش is also genuinely Yemeni (Watson; Q *maabiš* is the Sanaa variant, and Lisan attests 100). **Author guidance:** write it as two words **ما فيش** or as **مابش** so the detector (which matches the single token مافيش) does not reject correct Yemeni; and raise with the rulebook owner whether that bad-example should be narrowed to the fused Egyptian spelling only.

Sanaa system (Q Unit 18; Watson; corpus):

| | near | far |
|---|---|---|

| f. | **هذي / هاذي** hāði (L هذي 335, هاذي 31; هذه 1,625) ~ **ذي** ðī (L 209) | **هذيك** haðīk (L 9) ~ **ذيك** (L 12) |
| pl. | **هذول / ذول / هذولا / ذولا** hāðōl(ā) (L هذول 50, ذول 21, ذولا 25, هولا 26) | **هذولاك** haðawlāk (Q) |

- هذا قات صنعاني. — *hāða gāt ṣanʕānī* — "This is Sanaa qat."

Traps: هذا/هذه are **whitelisted** for Yemeni in the detector (Lisan 17:1 and 8:1) — they are fine in authored text; but voice them Yemeni: **هذي** rather than هذه, and show the short **ذا/ذي/ذول** as the "speak like Sanaa" upgrade. **ده/دي** (L 1,021/208) are Egyptian media leakage in Yemeni tweets — the corpus tool flags them; never teach them. **ذلك** (L 524) is on `UNIVERSAL_MSA_LEAKS` — use هذاك/ذاك.

#### 4.6 Imperatives (positive) — `verb-conjugation`
Sanaa inflects four forms (m., f., m.pl., f.pl.) — Q Unit 9/17: *tafaḍḍal / tafaḍḍalī / tafaḍḍalū / tafaḍḍalayn*; *ijlis / ijlisī / ijlisū / ijlisayn*; *kul / kulī / kulū / kulayn* "eat"; *uṭbux, uṭbuxī, uṭbuxū, uṭbuxayn* "cook".
- تفضّل اجلس! — *tafaḍḍal ijlis!* — "Please sit down." (Q Unit 9)
- هات واحد شاهي. — *hāt wāḥid šāhī* — "Bring one tea." (L هات 52; Aden: هبله واحد شاهي "give him a tea")
- تعال هنا! — *taʕāl hāna!* — "Come here!" (L تعال 135)
- خلّيك معنا. — *xallīk maʕna* — "Stay with us." (L خليك 178; خل 703 — "let")
- اسمع! — *ismaʕ!* (L 97); روح/سير! — "go!" (L روح 341); خزّن معنا. — *xazzin maʕna* — "Chew (qat) with us." (L خزن 36 imperative "chew")

Trap: **هيّا** in Sanaa is an attention-getter / uninflected imperative "hey! come!" (Q Unit 57: *hayya! māliš mugazzaza?* "Hey, what are you so grumpy about?"; *ʕalī, aštīk. hayya!* "Ali, I want you. Come!"; L هيا 72 "let's go!"). The repo rule "هيّا (not هيا بنا)" is right in spirit; don't gloss it as MSA "let's go" only.

#### 4.7 Negation I: nouns/adjectives with ماش / مش — `negation`
Q Unit 10 and Unit 59: **ماش** *māš* "always precedes a noun, an adjective, an adverb, or a phrase": هو ماش مهندس — *huw māš muhandis* — "He's not an engineer"; الجو ماش بارد — *al-jaww māš bārid* — "The weather isn't cold"; هي ماش هنا — "She isn't here"; هم ماش في المدرسة. Neither…nor: **ماش … وماش** (Q). Watson lists **miš/maš**; Transparent blog gives Yemeni **مَشْ**: أنا مَش موافق; Lisan: مش 1,326, مو 665, ماهو 221, ماهي 38.
- أنا مش جوعان. — *ana miš jawʕān* — "I'm not hungry." (Q: *ant māš jāwiʕ?* "Aren't you hungry?")
- الزلط مش معي. — *az-zalaṭ miš maʕī* — "The money's not on me." (Q)
- هذا مو صنعاني، هذا عدني. — *hāða mū ṣanʕānī* — "He's not from Sanaa, he's Adeni." (مو is Gulf-facing but whitelisted; مش/ماش is the safer default)

Trap: spell it **مش** (or **ماش**) in authored text — both are whitelisted; never **ليس**.

#### 4.8 Present (imperfect) with and without بـ — `verb-conjugation`
Paradigm (Q Unit 12–13, verb اشتى "want" and تكلّم):

| | -šti- "want" | -tkallam- "speak" |
|---|---|---|
| أنا | **أشتي** aštī | أتكلم atkallam |
| أنت | **تشتي** tištī | تتكلم titkallam |
| أنتي | **تشتي** tištī (fem. sg. = masc. in this verb; sound verbs add -ī: تدرسي) | تتكلمي |
| هو | **يشتي** yištī | يتكلم yitkallam |
| هي | **تشتي** tištī | تتكلم |
| إحنا | **نشتي** ništī | نتكلم |
| أنتو | **تشتو** tištū | تتكلمو |
| أنتين | تشتين tištayn | تتكلمين |
| هم | **يشتو** yištū (L يشتوا 59) | يتكلمو |
| هن | يشتين yištayn | يتكلمين |

Stem vowel: **يُدرس، يُكتب** (u-verbs), **يِشرب، يِسمع** (i-verbs) — Q. Sanaa 1sg is **أ-** (aktub); 3ms **يـ** (yiktub); no final -n on plurals (يروحو، يشتو).

The بـ question (this is where the Gulf design doc's "ب + verb for habitual" must not be copied blindly):
- **Bare imperfect** = habitual / generic / after modals: أتكلم عربي كل يوم — *atkallam ʕarabī kull yawm* — "I speak Arabic every day" (Q); يتكلم عربي ناهي — "he speaks Arabic well" (Q).
- **بـ + imperfect** = ongoing now / co-occurring: هو بيتكلم عربي ذلحين — *hū bi-yitkallam ʕarabī ðalḥīn* — "He is speaking Arabic now" (Q); واحد بيدرس وواحد بيكتب — "one is studying and another is writing" (Q Unit 18); علي بيبيع قات؛ بلقيس بتطبخ سلتة (S11).
- Watson (via S5): by the 1990s **bi-** was "generalizing" and appears "with stative verbs (Watson 1993:81)", i.e. it is becoming a plain present marker as in Egyptian/Levantine. Persson (S12) reports it "to express the progressive and possibly the habitual" in Sanaa.

**Teaching decision:** introduce the bare form first (A1, with modals and habits), add **بـ** for "right now" in the same lesson, and tell learners that in Sanaa today بـ is heard on habits too and is never wrong in a main clause; keep the bare form after اشتي/لازم/يقدر/عشان (Q's "double imperfect": أشتي أروح — *aštī arūḥ* — "I want to go"; نشتي ندرس عربي). In **Aden**, بـ is the *future* (S3 *ba-/bal-*), so a southern speaker's بأروح means "I'll go" — recognition note at Stage 3.

Trap for the detector: the Sanaa 3ms prefix is sometimes written **يِ** and the 1sg **أ**; a form like باشرب (L باشرب? — L بادرس 10, باكل 8, باعرف 17 tagged "future/study") is the Gulf/Ḥijāzi *bā-* 1sg — attested in Lisan but not Ṣanʿāni; write **أشرب / بأشرب** only if the 1sg بـ is intended.

#### 4.9 Want اشتي (and the southern/eastern alternatives) — `verb-conjugation`
- **اشتى / يشتي** is the Ṣanʿāni, Taʿizzi and Adeni verb (Q *-šti-*: aštī 55×; F *ashti* 44×; L اشتي 169 + يشتي 92 + نشتي 66 + تشتي 76 + يشتوا 59 ≈ 460). Past: **اشتيت** *aštayt* "I wanted"; "he wanted to" = **كان يشتي** (Q Unit 36 vocabulary).
- **بغيت / يبغى / أبغى** (L 35/34/27) is Ḥaḍrami and Gulf-facing (S8 lists بغيت under Ḥaḍrami); **ابي / يبي** (L 130/95) is Yāfiʿi/Gulf. All are whitelisted in the detector and in `YEMENI_IDENTITY`, so they are *tolerated*, but the curriculum should teach **اشتي** as the Yemeni form and mention بغيت as "what you'll hear from Ḥaḍramawt and the Gulf".
- اشتي أسير السوق. — *aštī asīr as-sūg* — "I want to go to the market." (Q: *aštī arūḥ la-fundug aš-šērātōn*)
- تشتي شاهي ولا قهوة؟ — *tištī šāhī walla gahwa?* — "Do you want tea or coffee?" (F Adeni: *tishti tishrab qahwa?*)
- ما نشتيش. — *mā ništīš* — "We don't want (to)." (Q Unit 59: *ma niftiiš*)
- Object suffix: أشتيك — *aštīk* — "I want you (m.)" (Q Unit 57); أشتيها — "I like it (f.)" (Q Unit 18: *'aštīha*).

Trap: the repo demonstration ends «بغيت نطلع نشرب شاهي؟» — grammatical Yemeni, but Sanaa-flavoured content should prefer «تشتي نطلع نشرب شاهي؟».

#### 4.10 Numbers 1–10 with nouns; dual — `sentence-structure`
Sanaa counting forms (Q Unit 8/13, Sanaa keeps θ): **واحد** wāḥid (f. **واحدة** wāḥideh), **ثنين** θnayn / iθnayn (f. **ثنتين** θintayn), **ثلاثة** θalāθa, **أربعة** arbaʕa, **خمسة** xamsa, **ستة** sitta, **سبعة** sabʕa, **ثمانية** θamāniya, **تسعة** tisʕa, **عشرة** ʕašara. Aden: *talāta, itnēn* (S3). 11–19: **حداعش / حداعشر** ḥdaʕš(ar), ثناعش, ثلاثطعش… (Q: "-aʕš/-ṭaʕš, or -aʕšar before a noun"). Tens: عشرين، ثلاثين… mixed: واحد وعشرين.

Rules (Q Unit 13):
- 1 and 2 **follow** the noun and agree: ولد واحد، بنت واحدة، بنتين ثنتين.
- 3–10 **precede** a **plural** noun — ثلاثة جهّال، أربعة فصول (*arbaʕat fuṣūl*) — except money stays singular: **خمسة ريال** *xamsa ryāl*, تسعة دينار.
- 11+ take a **singular** noun: حداعشر ولد، عشرين ريال، خمسين معلم.
- Dual **-ayn / -tayn** (Q): ريالين *ryālayn*, ساعتين *sāʕatayn*, سنتين, أختين, شركتين; nouns in -a take *-tayn*, nouns in -ā take *-yayn* (مستشفيين).
- كم + singular: كم ريال تشتي؟ — *kam ryāl tištī?* (Q).

Examples: عندي ثلاثة جهّال وبنتين. — "I have three kids and two girls." / الكيلو بمئة ريال. — *al-kīlō bi-miyat ryāl* — "A kilo is 100 riyals." / ثنين شاهي، لو سمحت. — "Two teas, please."

Trap: Sanaa **ثنين** (θ) not اتنين; do not write ٢ as إثنان. Yemeni riyal: ريال (L 167), pl. ريالات / colloquial invariable ريال after numbers.

#### 4.11 Ordinals, time, days, dates — `sentence-structure`
- Ordinals (Q Unit 14): **أول** awwal, **ثاني** θānī, **ثالث** θāliθ, **رابع** rābiʕ, **خامس** xāmis, **سادس** sādis, **سابع** sābiʕ, **ثامن** θāmin, **تاسع** tāsiʕ, **عاشر** ʕāšir; may precede an uninflected noun — أول معلمة *awwal muʕallima*, أول يومين.
- Time (Q Unit 8/9): drop the -a of the numeral: **الساعة خمس** *as-sāʕa xams*, الساعة عشر *ʕašir*, الساعة ثمان *θamān*, الساعة تسع *tisiʕ*, الساعة سبع *sabiʕ*; feminine one/two: **الساعة واحدة**، **الساعة ثنتين**; old-Sanaa construct **ساعة عشر** "at ten"; add ونص / وربع / إلا ربع [own knowledge, pan-Arabic]. "What time?" = **الساعة كم؟** or **في أي حين؟** (Q).
- Days (Q): **الجمعة، السبت، الأحد، الاثنين، الثلاثاء (الثلوث in Aden: yōm at-talūt, S3)، الأربعاء، الخميس**; **اليوم الجمعة** "today is Friday", **يوم الجمعة** "on Friday" (Q). Tomorrow **بكرة** *bukra* (L 225) / Sanaa **غدوة** *ġudwa* (Q 4×; schoolazal "غضوه = بكره"); yesterday **أمس** (L 156); day before yesterday **أول أمس**.
- Age (Q Unit 24): **عمري خمسين سنة** — *ʕumrī xamsīn sana*; **كم عمرك / عمرش؟**

#### 4.12 Future: شـ / عـ / عاد / بـ — `verb-conjugation`
- Q Unit 17: "The future tense … is indicated by prefixing **ša-** or **ʕa-** to the imperfect… In Sanaa **š-** is usually used with the first person singular; in other regions it is used with all persons." **عايجي** *ʕa-yijī* "he will come", **عايدرس** *ʕa-yudrus*, **شاكتب** *ša-ktub* "I'll write", **شاشتغل** *ša-štaġil* (1sg أ- drops). S6: 1sg **ša-** or **ʕād**, other persons **ʕa-**; villages use ša- for all. S11: عَـ commoner than شَـ; **عَيِشتري قات / شَيِشتري قات**. Ar-Wikipedia calls this "التشويف": شاعمل.
- **عاد** + imperfect (Watson; the Sana'ani features abstract lists "future marker a'ad"): **عاد أسير** *ʕād asīr* "I'm about to go / I'll go" [own knowledge for the exact gloss]. Note عاد's other jobs in 4.20.
- Bare imperfect with a time word is also future (Q: *bi-yitkallam ġudwa?* "will he speak tomorrow?").
- Negative: **ما شاتصلش لك** — *mā ša-ttaṣilš lak* — "I won't call you" (S11: ما شَأَتَّصِلش / ما عَأَتَّصِلش).
- Examples: شاسير السوق بكرة. — *ša-sīr as-sūg bukra* — "I'll go to the market tomorrow." / عانخزّن عند علي العصر. — *ʕa-nxazzin ʕind ʕalī l-ʕaṣr* — "We'll chew at Ali's this afternoon." / الضيوف عايجو اليوم؟ — *aḍ-ḍuyūf ʕa-yijū l-yawm?* — "Are the guests coming today?" (Q drill)

Traps: **سوف/سـ** are MSA leaks. Aden **بـ / بلـ** = future (F). Tihāma **شـ** for all persons (S7). Do not spell the future as a separate word «شا أروح»; attach: شاروح / شأروح.

#### 4.13 Possessive حق — `possessives`
Q Unit 20 & Unit 59: **N + حق + N / حق + suffix**: **الورشة حق حسن** *al-warša ḥagg ḥasan*, **السيارة حقي / حقهم**; can also precede: **حق علي البيت**, **حقنا البلاد** "our country"; predicative: **البيت حقي** "the house is mine", **هذا البيت حق علي**. Q: in Sanaa "It is **not inflected** for gender or number." South/Taʿizz uses **حقت** for feminine (L حقت 11; S8 حقتي) and Aden **حقِه** = "hers" (Marefa). L: حق 736, حقك 123, حقه 97, حقكم 84, حقنا 83, حقهم 51, حقي 23 (*markers*).

- الشاهي حق الضيوف. — *aš-šāhī ḥagg aḍ-ḍuyūf* — "The tea is for the guests."
- المفرج حق البيت فوق. — *al-mafraj ḥagg al-bayt fōg* — "The house's mafraj is upstairs."
Trap: **بتاع** (L بتاعهم 6) and **مال** (Aden مالِه) are non-Sanaa; teach حق only.

#### 4.14 Negation II: verbs with ما…ش; ما قد…ش; لا…ولا — `negation`
Q Unit 59: "A verb is made negative by using **ma** before the verb and suffixing **-š** … The use of -š is optional, although it is usually used": **ما اصطبحش** *ma ṣṭabaḥš* "he didn't have breakfast", **ما سرتش** *ma sirtš* "I didn't go", **ما نشتيش**, **ما تدرسش**. Same on عند / به: **ما عندكش جهال؟**, **مابش مئ**. Watson/S11 confirm mā…š for Ṣanʿāni; L *ma…š* circumfix 122 tokens (README). Lisan's imperative-negation with **لـ** (*لتخافون* for لا تخافوا, paper cited in docs) is a *written* Yemeni feature — recognise, don't teach.
- ما شفتش أحد. — *mā šuftš aḥad* — "I didn't see anyone."
- ما نخزّنش رمضان النهار. — *mā nxazzinš ramaḍān an-nahār* — "We don't chew in the daytime in Ramadan."
- **محّد** *maḥḥad* "no one" (Q Unit 18 = ma ʔaḥad): محّد يعرفني هناك — *maḥḥad yiʕrifnī hunāk* — "Nobody knows me there." (Q)
- **لا … ولا** "neither…nor" and **ولا** "and not / nor" (Q Unit 59): لا علي ولا محمد; proverb **نار صنعاء ولا جنة ذمار** — "the hell of Sanaa rather than the paradise of Dhamar" (Q).
- Adverb **أبداً** *abadan* (L 162): ما رحتش أبداً.

Trap: the detector's forbidden list now contains the fused spellings مافيش/ماكانش/ماعنديش. Write **ما كانش**, **ما عنديش**, **ما فيش** with a space. Never **لا + noun** for "isn't" (that's لا = "no"); never ليس/لست.

#### 4.15 Modals لازم / ممكن / يقدر — `verb-conjugation`, `sentence-structure`
- **لازم** (L 312) + bare imperfect; Sanaa flavour **لازم على + suffix**: **لازم عليّ أسير ذلحين** *lāzim ʕalayy asīr ðalḥīn* "I have to go now" (Q Unit 22); **لازم عليك تروح**; negative **ما لازم عليّ أسير** "I don't have to go" vs **لازم عليّ ما أجيش اليوم** "I mustn't come today" (Q).
- **ممكن** (L 269) + imperfect: ممكن أشرب مئ؟ — "May I drink water?"
- **قدر / يقدر** (Q Unit 17 *-gdar-*; L يقدر 99, اقدر 28): نعم أقدر؛ **يقدر يروح**؛ **يقدر يروح يشرب** "he can go and drink" (Q); ما أقدرش (L مااقدرش 3).
- **يمكن** (L 263) = "maybe".
Trap: the Gulf يبي/يبغى-style "يبغى له" is not Yemeni; use لازم.

#### 4.16 Comparative / superlative — `sentence-structure`
Q Unit 22–23: elative **أَفْعَل**: جديد→**أجدد**, طويل→**أطول**, صعب→**أصعب**, رخيص→**أرخص**, نظيف→**أنظف**, بارد→**أبرد**, سهل→**أسهل**, غالي→**أغلى**, حلو→**أحلى**; with **من**: صنعاء أبرد من عدن — *ṣanʕā abrad min ʕadan* — "Sanaa is colder than Aden"; superlative with definite noun or **أ… واحد**: **أغلى قات في السوق** "the most expensive qat in the market"; **أحسن** "better/best" (L احسن high) ; **أكثر** (L 907 *markers*). Example: القات حق حراز أحسن من قات الحيمة. — "Ḥarāz qat is better than al-Ḥayma qat."

#### 4.17 كان + noun/adjective; كان + imperfect (used to / was doing) — `verb-conjugation`
Q Unit 30/36: **كان يشتي** "he wanted to"; **كان فيه**. Paradigm: كنت، كنت، كنتي، كان، كانت، كنا، كنتو، كانو (L كان 3,285, كنت 593, كانت 785).
- كنت في عدن السنة اللي فاتت. — *kunt fī ʕadan as-sana llī fātat* — "I was in Aden last year."
- كان يسير المدرسة كل يوم. — *kān yasīr al-madrasa kull yawm* — "He used to go to school every day."
- كانت بتطبخ لما جيت. — *kānat bi-tuṭbux lammā jīt* — "She was cooking when I came."
- Negative: **ما كانش** / **ما كنتش هنا**.

#### 4.23 Relative clause اللي — `sentence-structure`

Trap: Qafisheh's older informants used **اللذي** (a classical survival); it collides with the detector (الذي). Teach **اللي** only.

#### 4.24 Conditionals إذا / لو / (لا) — `sentence-structure`
Q Unit 40: open condition **إذا** *ʔiða* + perfect or imperfect, main clause perfect/imperfect/imperative: **إذا أبصرته قلت له / أقول له / قل له** "if I see him I'll tell him". Unlikely/unreal: **لو** *law* + perfect, main clause **كان** + perfect: **لو أبصرته كان قلت له** "if I had seen him I'd have told him". Sanaa also uses **لا** *lā* = "if" (Q: *lā falat min yaddī ṭār* "if it slips from my hand it flies") — a classical-flavoured Sanaa usage; recognise only, because لا = "no/don't" for learners. L: اذا 1,587, لو 2,297, لوما "if not" 30. **إن** as "if" is heard (L 6,284, but mostly "that").
- إذا جيت صنعاء، لازم تجرّب السلتة. — *ʔiða jīt ṣanʕā, lāzim tjarrib as-salta*
- لو كان معي زلط كان اشتريت قات حرازي. — "If I'd had money I'd have bought Ḥarāzi qat." (cf. F Adeni: *law kān maʕī fulūs kunt ba-štarī…*)
- إذا ما جاش، ما نخزّنش. — "If he doesn't come, we won't chew."

#### 4.25 Plurals (sound, broken, collective) and agreement — `sentence-structure`
Q Units 16–18, 37: sound **-īn/-āt** (معلمين، سيارات), broken plurals learned as vocabulary (جاهل→جهّال، بيت→بيوت، رجّال→رجاجيل [own knowledge], ولد→أولاد), **collective + unit noun -a** (Q: **قات** coll., unit **عود قات** *ʕūd gāt*; بطاطس/بطاطسة, تفاح/تفاحة, تمر/تمرة, خيار/خيارة); adjective agreement — plural humans take plural adjective, non-humans feminine singular or plural (Q Unit 18 "noun-adjective concord"); dual nouns take plural adjectives (Q Unit 37).

#### 4.26 Word order, topic–comment, and the vocative يا — `sentence-structure`
Q Unit 10 "topical sentences": **الولد اسمه حسين** *al-walad ʔisma ḥusayn* "the boy's name is Hussein", **علي أخته سكرتيرة**; vocative **يا** (Q Unit 5): **يا حاج أحمد!**, **يا شيخ**, **يا عم**. Verb–subject order is flexible; questions keep intonation.

#### 4.27 Reported/embedded speech and إنه — `sentence-structure`
**قال إنه…**, **أعرف إنه ما فيش وقت** (F: *ana aʕrif annuh mā fish waqt*), **أظن إنه جاي**. B1 also gets **عشان + imperfect** purpose clauses and **قبل ما / بعد ما** + verb.
